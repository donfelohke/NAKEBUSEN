## 2. Tax invoicing — KRA eTIMS

KRA's published eTIMS guidance states that persons engaged in business are generally required to onboard and issue electronic tax invoices. KRA also provides simplified solutions for non-VAT-registered taxpayers and a buyer-initiated invoicing process for qualifying purchases from small-scale suppliers.

**Operational implications to verify with KRA or a qualified tax adviser:**

1. Identify the correct taxpayer/business identity and applicable tax obligations.
2. Confirm the appropriate eTIMS solution and onboarding steps.
3. Confirm how services, deposits, refunds, corrections and customer receipts should be represented.
4. Keep business records and invoices in the manner required by current rules.
5. Do not interpret the buyer-initiated invoicing facility as a blanket exemption from a seller's own obligations.
6. Do not build automated payment or tax integrations before the legal, accounting and operational requirements are understood.

Official sources:
- KRA eTIMS overview: https://www.kra.go.ke/online-services/etims
- KRA eTIMS FAQ: https://www.kra.go.ke/helping-tax-payers/faqs/learn-about-etims
- KRA buyer-initiated invoicing service: https://www.kra.go.ke/services/service/16

The threshold and eligibility conditions for buyer-initiated invoicing must be checked against the current KRA rules. The small-supplier facility is not a general waiver of electronic invoicing obligations.

## 3. Personal data and ODPC

A beauty and loc-care practice may handle personal data through enquiries, WhatsApp conversations, appointment records, payment references, service notes and photographs.

Treat the business as responsible for understanding its role as a data controller when it determines why and how customer information is used. A third party handling information on the business's behalf may be a processor. Actual roles depend on the processing arrangement.

### 3.1 Registration is a threshold-and-sector question

ODPC guidance describes an exemption from mandatory registration where BOTH conditions are met:
- annual turnover/revenue is below KES 5 million; AND
- the entity has fewer than ten employees.

ODPC also lists categories that are not exempt regardless of those thresholds, including certain direct-marketing, hospitality, transport, health, education, financial and other activities. The business must check the current official list and its actual processing purposes. Do not assume a salon is automatically included in or excluded from a category based on its name alone.

Even where an entity is exempt from mandatory registration, the ODPC guidance says the substantive data-protection obligations under the Act continue to apply.

Official sources:
- ODPC registration FAQs: https://www.odpc.go.ke/faqs/
- ODPC compliance guidance: https://www.odpc.go.ke/data-protection-compliance/index.html
- Data Protection (Registration of Data Controllers and Data Processors) Regulations, 2021: https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-REGISTRATION-OF-DATA-CONTROLLERS-AND-DATA-PROCESSORS-REGULATIONS-2021.pdf
- ODPC Guidance Note on Processing by MSMEs: https://odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ODPC-%E2%80%93-Guidance-Note-on-Processing-by-MSMEs.pdf

### 3.2 Minimum practical controls

- Collect only information needed to respond to an enquiry, arrange a service or meet a legal obligation.
- Explain why information is requested and how it will be used.
- Obtain separate, informed permission before taking or publishing identifiable client photographs.
- Do not treat consent to receive a service as consent to marketing or public image use.
- Restrict access to customer messages, contact details, appointment information and payment references.
- Keep private customer records out of public GitHub repositories and public research notes.
- Set a retention and deletion process; do not retain personal data indefinitely by default.
- Use device locks, account security and backups that do not expose client records.
- If a third party processes customer information, document the purpose, access and security expectations.

## 5. Service quality and operational evidence

The business should maintain a private operational record that can support improvement without turning customer information into a public dataset.

Potential non-identifying measures:
- enquiry-to-booking conversion;
- service duration by service type;
- material consumption and direct costs;
- rebooking and referral rates;
- cancellations and no-shows;
- customer-reported satisfaction;
- rework and aftercare follow-up;
- available working capacity.

Do not invent benchmarks. Establish a baseline from real records, explain the observation period and exclude personally identifying details from public summaries.

For hygiene and service procedures, validate each instruction against the products actually used, manufacturer guidance and relevant professional/public-health requirements. A written SOP is not proof that a procedure has been followed or independently audited.
