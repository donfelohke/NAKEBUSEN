## 2. County business permit: primary operational route

The County Government of Uasin Gishu operates a [County Revenue Management Self Service Portal](https://revenue.uasingishu.go.ke/). Its public service categories include Single Business Permits and a broad category describing general trade, shops, personal services and service providers.

The Government of Kenya's [eProcedures entry for Uasin Gishu single business permits](https://eprocedures.investkenya.go.ke/procedure/378/step/2038?embed=true&includeSearch=true&l=en) describes the county revenue management portal as the online application route, identifies the permit as calendar-year based, and lists applicant identity/business registration, KRA PIN and location/contact information among the requested details.

The City of Eldoret's [business permit service page](https://eldoretcity.go.ke/services/apply-for-business-permit-eldoret) directs businesses to confirm their activity category and fee through the County revenue workflow or licensing desk. It does not establish NAKEBUSEN's current permit status.

**Required owner action:**
1. Ask the County Revenue / City of Eldoret licensing desk to classify the actual activity (natural loc care, retouch/styling and Sisterlocs retie) under the current approved schedule.
2. Confirm the applicable municipality/sub-county, premises and employee-count bands.
3. Request the current Finance Act schedule and written assessment; do not use historic eProcedures estimates or a generic personal-services category as the payable fee.
4. Confirm any separate premises, fire, signage, public-health or waste-related approvals that apply to the actual premises and service scope.
5. Keep the issued permit and receipts privately; publish no ID, PIN, plot or permit identifiers.

The county portal's category descriptions and fee tables are evidence of the portal's published categories, not a ruling on the correct classification of this business. The current Finance Act and written county assessment control the actual fee.

## 4. Public health and premises hygiene

The [Public Health Act, Cap. 242](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/1921/38/eng%402022-12-31) is the relevant national public-health statutory framework. The exact provisions, current amendments, county implementation and any applicable subsidiary rules must be checked before making a specific legal claim.

Do not rely on an unattributed or non-Kenyan “Public Health Act 2009” text that contains salon-specific provisions. It is not the Kenyan Public Health Act, Cap. 242, and must not be used to assert that a particular checklist, six-month medical examination, antiseptic rule or salon inspection requirement is Kenyan law without a verified Kenyan legal source.

**Practical operating controls to document, subject to product instructions and county guidance:**
- hand hygiene before and after each client;
- clean work surfaces and client-contact areas between appointments;
- separate clean, used and waste tools/materials;
- use single-use items once and dispose of them safely;
- clean reusable tools using a documented method appropriate to the tool and material;
- follow manufacturer dilution, contact-time, storage and expiry instructions for cleaning/disinfection products;
- label and safely store chemicals; retain safety data sheets where supplied;
- avoid sharing products in ways that contaminate the container;
- maintain ventilation and safe electrical-tool use where applicable;
- record incidents, product reactions reported by clients and corrective action without making diagnoses.

These are proposed service-quality controls, not a substitute for an inspection checklist or a statement that every item is a statutory requirement. Product instructions and competent public-health guidance take precedence over invented recipes or concentrations.

## 5. Occupational safety

The [Occupational Safety and Health Act, 2007](https://www.labour.go.ke/laws-regulations) is the national workplace-safety reference. Determine its application to the actual premises, staffing and work arrangements with DOSHS or qualified Kenyan advice.

Risk-assessment topics include electrical equipment, trip hazards, ventilation, chemical storage and handling, manual handling, fire response, first aid and incident recording. Do not claim that a particular registration, inspection or medical-surveillance requirement applies without checking the Act and relevant regulations against the actual business.

## 6. Hair and scalp claims: professional boundary

The American Academy of Dermatology's public guidance discusses traction risk from tight hairstyles. A peer-reviewed study on marginal traction alopecia is population- and study-specific. Neither source authorises diagnosis by a loctician or a blanket claim that locs cause hair loss.

Operational boundary:
- avoid unnecessarily tight tension and painful styling;
- ask the client to communicate discomfort during the service;
- do not diagnose scalp or hair disorders;
- recommend a qualified health professional when a client reports persistent pain, inflammation, sudden shedding or other concerning symptoms;
- do not promise prevention, treatment or cure.

References: [AAD hairstyle/traction guidance](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/hair-loss/causes/hairstyles); [study record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18694677/).

## 7. Customer data and photographs

The Data Protection Act 2019 and ODPC guidance remain relevant to customer enquiries, contact details, appointment notes, payment references and identifiable photographs.

Minimum operating rules:
- collect only what is needed for the stated service or legal purpose;
- provide a concise privacy explanation at the point of collection;
- keep service consent separate from marketing and image-publication consent;
- obtain explicit, documented permission before publishing identifiable client images;
- specify channel, purpose, duration and whether edits/derivatives are permitted;
- keep private client records and identity documents out of public GitHub;
- establish access controls, retention and deletion;
- check current ODPC registration applicability against actual turnover, employee count, processing purposes and the current statutory exemptions.

An open-source licence, public social-media post or public GitHub repository does not substitute for client consent or rights clearance.
