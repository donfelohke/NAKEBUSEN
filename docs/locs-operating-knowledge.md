# NAKEBUSEN LOCS — Operating Knowledge

**Purpose:** A structured starting point for documenting loc-care practice, service quality and client communication. This is an operating framework, not a claim that every procedure has already been approved or validated.

## Service principles

- Respect the client's requested style, comfort, time, budget and informed choices.
- Explain what a service includes and what may require a separate quote before work begins.
- Distinguish retouch/styling, Sisterlocs retie/maintenance and artificial loc installation; they are not interchangeable services.
- Never promise a particular result without examining the hair, loc condition, requested style, materials and time required.
- Do not claim a technique is suitable for every client. Escalate uncertainty and avoid work outside the practitioner's competence.
- Obtain permission before taking or publishing client photographs. A client enquiry or service does not imply marketing consent.

## Consultation record — minimum useful questions

Ask only what is necessary to plan the requested service:

1. Which service is requested: retouch and styling, Sisterlocs retie, artificial loc installation, or another service confirmed by the practitioner?
2. What is the current condition, approximate length and density of the locs, and when was the last maintenance?
3. What style, finish, timing and budget does the client have in mind?
4. Are there sensitivities, discomfort, scalp concerns, product constraints or other conditions that should affect the service? Do not diagnose; when a concern is outside the practitioner's competence, pause and recommend appropriate professional advice.
5. Which materials, preparation and additional work might be required?
6. What estimate, duration, location and availability have been discussed?

Use the approved private operating method for any necessary appointment record. Do not place completed client records in this repository.

## Before the service

- Confirm the agreed service, scope, estimated time and quote.
- Explain relevant limitations, maintenance expectations and any additional costs before proceeding.
- Check that tools, work area and products are suitable and prepared according to applicable manufacturer instructions and professional hygiene guidance.
- Ask about comfort and preferences; do not proceed with a method that causes unreasonable pain or appears to risk injury.
- Confirm permission before changing the agreed scope or taking photographs.

## During the service

- Work within training and demonstrated competence.
- Use tools and products according to their instructions; avoid improvising chemical or mechanical procedures where safety is uncertain.
- Check client comfort and the developing result.
- Pause if pain, irritation, unexpected breakage, a concerning scalp condition or another risk appears. Do not diagnose or conceal an adverse event.
- Discuss any proposed change in scope and cost before carrying it out.

## Before the client leaves

- Review the result with the client and note any agreed follow-up privately if needed.
- Explain only aftercare that is appropriate to the service and materials used; verify product-specific instructions rather than inventing them.
- Confirm any maintenance recommendation as guidance, not a guarantee.
- Invite feedback without pressuring the client to provide a testimonial or public image.
- If a concern arises, document it through the controlled operating process and handle it respectfully.

## Price and booking controls

The repository catalogue is the review record; the live public offer must be checked before use. Known website starting prices have been presented as KES 700 for Retouch & Styling, KES 1,500 for Sisterlocs Retie, and KES 3,500 for Artificial Locs Install, but the catalogue flags current owner confirmation as required. Do not treat these figures as freshly reconfirmed by this document.

State that prices are starting prices, explain factors affecting the quote, and confirm the final scope and price directly. An enquiry is not a booking; availability and appointment details require direct confirmation.

## Quality and incident review

For a procedure or quality incident, record in a restricted system—not this public repository—the minimum necessary facts, what was observed, what action was taken, what the client was told, and any follow-up. Avoid unnecessary personal or medical details. Review recurring problems for improvements to the procedure, materials or consultation.

## Evidence needed before this becomes an approved SOP

- Practitioner review and approval of the actual methods used.
- Current product/tool list and applicable manufacturer guidance.
- Hygiene and sanitation procedure grounded in appropriate professional guidance and local requirements.
- Clear escalation process for conditions outside scope of practice.
- Owner-approved aftercare for each service type.
- Date, reviewer and revision history.
