# Before Africa — Naming, Heritage and Identity

**NAKEBUSEN research note · 7 November 2026 · Status: RESEARCH**

## Why this belongs here

NAKEBUSEN's public proposition, “Beautiful Roots. Sovereign Systems,” invites serious engagement with heritage and cultural identity. This note establishes a research boundary: celebrate specific living cultures and histories with attribution; do not turn a complex continent into a single aesthetic, generic motif or unsupported origin story.

## Naming and self-identification

The etymology of “Africa” remains disputed. Roman use of *Africa* for a North African region is historically documented, while the expansion of the term to the whole continent occurred over a long period. Earlier and concurrent geographical labels such as Libya, Aethiopia, Egypt and Nubia referred to particular places or regions; they were not universal names for all peoples.

Do not state that “Alkebulan” is conclusively the oldest indigenous name for the whole continent. Do not treat Kenya, Turkana, Amazigh, Kemet, Kush, Yoruba, Swahili or any other identity as interchangeable. Every cultural term requires language, community, place, period and source.

## Heritage and representation standards

- Identify the specific community, maker, language, place and context behind a visual or cultural reference.
- Distinguish community self-description from outsider labels and commercial interpretation.
- Obtain consent and permission for identifiable people, client images, sacred motifs and restricted cultural materials.
- Do not use cultural identity as a decorative shorthand or imply endorsement by a community.
- Credit artists, artisans, knowledge holders and source custodians.
- Separate verified etymology from founder interpretation and campaign language.
