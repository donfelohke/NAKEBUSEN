## Why this belongs here

NAKEBUSEN is grounded in physical practice, beauty, heritage, craft and enterprise. This note establishes how the business may engage African histories and living cultural identities without turning diverse peoples into a single aesthetic, unsupported origin story or commercial shorthand.

## Research and representation rules

- Identify the specific community, language, maker, place, period and context behind a cultural reference.
- Distinguish community self-description from outsider labels, colonial categories and commercial interpretation.
- Treat hair, adornment, dress, beauty and craft as situated practices with changing meanings—not as universal symbols.
- Do not infer ethnicity, ancestry, identity, status or cultural permission from appearance alone.
- Credit makers, artists, knowledge holders, photographers, archives and source custodians.
- Obtain appropriate consent and rights for identifiable people, client images, sacred motifs and restricted materials.
- Separate verified etymology and historical evidence from founder interpretation, brand language and campaign copy.
- Do not imply endorsement by a community or claim that a current style is unchanged from prehistory without evidence.

## Naming and history

The etymology and geographical history of “Africa” require source-specific treatment. Roman use of Africa for a North African region is documented; the extension of the term to the whole continent occurred over time. Kemet, Kush, Nubia, Amazigh, Turkana, Swahili, Yoruba and other names refer to distinct historical, linguistic, geographic or cultural contexts. They are not interchangeable.

“Alkebulan” may be documented as a contemporary identity expression and a historical claim to investigate, but it must not be marketed as the proven universal ancient name of the continent.

“Beautiful Roots. Sovereign Systems.” is NAKEBUSEN's adopted brand proposition. Brand storytelling is not independent historical evidence.
